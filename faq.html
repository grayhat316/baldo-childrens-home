Questions people ask

Last revised 1 November 2026. Written for the demonstration site.

Is this the home's official website?

No. It is a demonstration built from the home's own documents and from published accounts by its partners. The home has not adopted it and it takes no money.

How many children live there?

Around a hundred. The sources differ because they were written in different years: one document says 130, another about 100, a newspaper report from the year the Rotary funding ended says 161, and a British charity's page says about 80. Ask the home for today's number.

Who runs it?

The Congregation of the Little Daughters of St. Joseph, since 2000. It was founded in 1993 by parishioners of Ndaragwa Catholic Parish under Fr. Gerald Mathenge, and the parish still holds it.

Can I sponsor one child?

That is a conversation with the sisters, not a checkout, because it involves a child's privacy. Call or write and ask.

Can I visit?

Yes, and there is a guest house on the compound. Call first on 0710 238 132 so the home knows who is coming.

Where does my money go?

School fees, food the farm does not produce, medicine, uniforms, water and electricity, and farm inputs. The money page explains why no figures are published here.

Is my donation tax deductible?

Not promised. It depends on the home's registration and on the law where you pay tax. There are partner organisations in Italy and Britain that may be able to issue local paperwork. Ask before giving a large amount.

Why did Rotary stop?

The ten year agreement expired. The Standard reported that the home was on the brink of closure as a result. Other supporters, mostly Italian and British, have continued.

What does the home need most?

By its own paperwork: a new kitchen, school uniforms, and fees to keep children in secondary school. Water is the constant constraint behind all of it, with supply reaching the compound about twice a week.

Who took the photographs?

Esharelife Foundation, a British charity that supports the home, which published them on its own site. They are credited on this one and would be replaced by the home's own photographs, with consent, on adoption.
