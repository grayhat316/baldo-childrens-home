Home / Education

School from nursery to university, and a trade for those who want one

The home pays fees the whole way through. It also pays for the courses that are not university, which is where a good number of these children end up.

The ladder

  1. Nursery, on the compoundFor children between three and five, before they go out to school.
  2. Primary and secondaryAt the state schools nearby. The home pays fees, uniform, books and term supplies.
  3. College or universityFor those who get there. Fees continue after eighteen.
  4. Or a tradeCarpentry, embroidery and masonry, taught to the young people not heading for a degree.

What education costs the home

Fees
Three terms a year, per child, at state school rates
Uniform and books
Needed at the start of every year, and again when a child grows
Boarding supplies
For the children at boarding schools
Scholarships
14 school leavers funded into advanced training by Rotary

Why uniforms keep appearing on the list

A child at a day school needs more than fees: a uniform, books, and something to carry them in. When the home is short of money, this is the first place it shows.

It is also the cheapest thing to fix. A uniform is a uniform, and it does not need a committee.

Give to school fees or uniforms

After school

Rotary funded scholarships for school leavers going on to advanced training. Fourteen young people have used them.

The home also runs career guidance in the April, August and December holidays, for children from upper primary upwards: which subjects to take, what the paths actually are, what the work looks like.

For those looking for work there is help with a CV and a cover letter, and the home takes on young people for attachment and volunteer placements where it can.
