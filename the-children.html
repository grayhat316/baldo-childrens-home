The children

Around a hundred children live at the home. The youngest are babies. The oldest are in college or university, or learning a trade.

They come for reasons that are not unusual in this part of Kenya. A mother dies and there is no one. A child is found alone in Ndaragwa town. A family cannot feed another mouth. The local authorities bring some of them, the parish brings others.

What we do not put on this website

No names. No faces with names attached. No story told in a child's own words unless that child is now an adult and has agreed to it.

This is deliberate. A children's home website that lists children invites the wrong kind of attention, and a child who is later reintegrated with family should not have to explain a page that is still online about them. The photographs on this site show groups taken from a distance, and are credited to the partner charity that took them.

If you want to know about an individual child, ask the home directly. That conversation can happen, carefully, and not in public.

What happens to a child who arrives

The first thing is usually health. Many arrive malnourished, and some arrive HIV positive. The home gets them onto medication and treatment, and works with Ndaragwa Health Centre on testing and follow up.

Then school. There is a nursery on the compound for the three to five year olds. Older children go to the state schools nearby, and the home covers the fees, the uniform and the books. After secondary there is college, university or a vocational course. The home does not stop paying at eighteen.

Then family. Where there is a relative who can take the child, the sisters work towards getting the child back to that family and follow up afterwards. Where there is nobody, and where it is in the child's interest, adoption is looked at instead.

Alongside that there is the ordinary business of a childhood: sport, drama, music, a talent day, films for the little ones, and career guidance in the school holidays for those old enough to need it.

The cost of one child

School fees, a uniform, three meals, medicine, and a place in a dormitory that needs water and electricity. The farm covers part of the food. The rest is bought.

If you want the current figure for one child for one year, ask the home and the sisters will give it to you. We would rather you had the real number than one invented for a web page.
