Home / The children

Around a hundred children, and not one of them named here

The youngest are babies. The oldest are in college or university, or learning a trade. This page explains who comes, what happens on arrival, and why you will not find a child's name on it.

Who comes here

Found on the street

Brought in by the local authorities after being found alone in Ndaragwa or the towns around it.

Orphaned by AIDS

Children who lost parents in the years when the illness emptied whole households in this part of Nyandarua.

Abandoned as babies

Left with no one able to claim them. The home has a nursery for the smallest of these.

Abused or neglected

Children who cannot stay where they are, including victims of physical, emotional or sexual abuse.

What happens when a child arrives

  1. Health firstMany arrive malnourished. Some arrive HIV positive. The home starts treatment and works with Ndaragwa Health Centre on testing and follow up.
  2. A bed and a routineOne of the four pavilions, a uniform, and the fact of knowing where the next meal is coming from.
  3. SchoolNursery on the compound for the little ones, the state schools nearby for the rest. Fees, books and uniform paid by the home.
  4. Family, if there is anyThe sisters trace relatives and work towards reintegration, then follow up after the child has gone home.
  5. Adoption, if there is notWhere reunification is not in the child's interest, adoption is explored instead.

Children's privacy

No names. No face attached to a name. No story told in a child's own words unless that child is now an adult and has agreed to it.

This is deliberate. A child who is later reintegrated with family should not have a permanent public page about the hardest year of their life, so the home does not identify the children in its care.

If you want to know about an individual child, ask the home directly. That conversation can happen, carefully, and not in public.

What one child costs

School fees, a uniform, three meals a day, medicine, and a place in a dormitory that needs water and electricity. The farm covers part of the food; the rest is bought.

For the current figure for one child for one year, ask the home and the sisters will give it to you.
