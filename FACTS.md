## Name and status
- Fr. Baldo Children's Home (Rehabilitation Center), Ndaragwa — home's own letterhead
- Registered Charitable Community Based Organization (CBO)
- Under the umbrella of Ndaragwa Catholic Parish
- Run by the Congregation of the Little Daughters of St. Joseph (Piccole Figlie di S. Giuseppe, Verona)
- Italian donors call it "Casa Maria"

## History
- 1993: started as an initiative of Christians of good will under Fr. Gerald Mathenge, then
  priest in charge of Ndaragwa Parish. Motive: refuge for orphans and street children orphaned
  or neglected during the HIV/AIDS pandemic
- 2000: handed over to the Little Daughters of St. Joseph for management
- 2004: dormitory inaugurated with funds raised by Rotary District 2030 (~EUR 450,000), under
  then-Governor Sebastiano Cocuzza
- 2014: solar panels and rebuilt irrigation for the farm (Rotary Districts 2030/2031 + Rotary Foundation)
- 2020: minibus bought to connect children to local schools (Rotary Turin Castello, Auxerre,
  Alzenau + InsiemeperDonare)
- November (year reported by The Standard): Rotary International withdrew after a ten-year
  support agreement expired. The Standard reported the home "on the brink of closure", 161
  children at that time

## Children
- Children abandoned or found living on the street; orphaned through HIV/AIDS; victims of
  physical, emotional or sexual abuse; other destitute children
- Local authorities help identify and rescue children
- Newborns up to about 15 years at intake
- Older children are in ECD, primary, secondary, college, vocational training and university

## How it works
- Reintegration first: reunites children with family/relatives where possible; adoption explored
  when reunification is not in the child's interest
- Education is paid for across the full ladder, including university and vocational training
- Healthcare, including medication and treatment for children who are HIV positive
- Mixed farm: maize, wheat, vegetables, onions, cabbages, sukuma wiki, spinach; pigs, cattle,
  chicken, rabbits. Produce feeds the home, surplus sold at the nearby market to cover water and
  electricity bills
- Technical training: carpentry, embroidery, masonry
- Clothes sewn and repaired within the home
- Four pavilions plus a nursery; electric bread oven; hot and cold water; guest house for visitors
  and volunteers
- Microcredit for local women, run by Sister Peris: 32 loans granted, all repaid except one
- Scholarships for school leavers: 14 delivered for advanced training
- Outreach with Ndaragwa Health Centre: HIV testing and counselling, nutrition training
- Women and youth skills: soap making, basket weaving, khaki bags, beadwork, shaggy mats,
  silage, dairy goats, potato farming, kitchen and sack gardening, CV writing, job linkage
