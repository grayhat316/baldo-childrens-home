Home / Outreach

The work outside the gate, so fewer children need a home

Most of what the home does beyond its own children is aimed at the women and young people of Ndaragwa.

Four programmes

Skills that earn

Soap making, basket weaving, khaki bags, beadwork and shaggy mats. Not certificates: small businesses.

Farming

Silage, dairy goats, cattle, potato growing and storage, kitchen and sack gardens, rabbits and chickens.

Health

Nutrition training and HIV testing with Ndaragwa Health Centre, mostly for single mothers and young people.

Microcredit

Small loans for women with no access to credit. 32 made, all but one repaid, and the money lent out again.

The lending scheme that keeps working

A small fund was set up for women in the community who had no access to credit at all, to start a farm plot or a craft business. Sister Peris ran it.

Thirty two loans have been made. All but one came back. Money repaid is lent to someone else, which is how the fund has stayed alive without new donations.

It is the part of the work that needs the least money, and probably does the most good.

Health, with the health centre

Nutrition training for mothers covers balanced diets, healthy cooking, breastfeeding in the first six months, and the conditions that come with poor nutrition. Testing and counselling is offered mostly to single mothers and young people. Those who test positive are referred on for further tests; those who test negative get counselling on staying that way.

Work

For young people looking for employment, the home runs workshops on writing a CV and a cover letter, and links people to opportunities where it can. It also takes on young people for attachment and volunteer placements.
