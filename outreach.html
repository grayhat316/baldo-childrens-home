Outreach

The home does not stop at its own gate. Most of what it does outside is aimed at the women and young people of Ndaragwa, so that fewer children end up needing a home in the first place.

Skills that earn

Soap making, basket weaving, khaki bag making, beadwork and shaggy mats are taught to women and youth in the area. The point is not a certificate, it is a small business. Some of the groups have started one.

Alongside that is farming: silage making, dairy goats, cattle, and modern methods of growing and storing potatoes. Kitchen gardens and sack gardens for households with very little land. Small scale rabbit and chicken keeping.

Health, with the health centre

The home works with Ndaragwa Health Centre on nutrition training for mothers and on HIV testing and counselling, mostly for single mothers and young people. Those who test positive are referred on for further tests. Those who test negative get counselling on staying that way. The training covers balanced diets, healthy cooking, breastfeeding in the first six months, and the conditions that come with poor nutrition.

Microcredit

A small lending scheme was set up for women in the community who had no access to credit at all, to start farming or a craft business. Sister Peris ran it. Thirty two loans have been made, and all but one came back. Money repaid can be borrowed again by someone else, which is how the fund has stayed alive without new donations.

Work

For young people looking for employment, the home runs workshops on writing a CV and a cover letter, and links people to opportunities where it can. It also takes on young people for attachment and volunteer placements.
