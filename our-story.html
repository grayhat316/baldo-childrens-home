Our story

The home began in 1993, in the years when AIDS was taking parents out of whole households in Nyandarua. It was started by parishioners of Ndaragwa Catholic Parish under Fr. Gerald Mathenge, who was the priest in charge, and registered as a community based organisation.

The first children were orphans and children living on the street. Some were brought by the local authorities, who still bring children today when they find one with nowhere to go.

In 2000 the parish handed management to the Congregation of the Little Daughters of St. Joseph. The sisters have run the home since, and the parish still holds it as its own work.

The buildings came later than the work

For the first decade the home made do. The dormitories were built in 2004, after Rotary District 2030 in Italy raised about 450,000 euros under its governor at the time, Sebastiano Cocuzza, a paediatrician who had worked in Somalia and then in Kenya. Clubs from Piedmont, Liguria and Valle d'Aosta put money in, and the Rotaract clubs made it their national project. The nursery was built with funds from one member, the students' residence with funds from another.

What stands there now is four pavilions and a nursery, a bread oven, a farm with cattle and rabbits and chickens, and a guest house for the people who come to visit or to help.

In 2014 the roofs got solar panels and the irrigation on the farm was rebuilt. In 2020 a minibus was bought so the children could get to the schools in the area.

The years since

The Italian support has been steady. Ruota Amica manages donations for the home. InsiemeperDonare has worked in Ndaragwa for twenty years. Nati per Lottare has sponsored school fees for about forty children through its friends of Gabriella group. In Britain, the Esharelife Foundation has funded projects at the home and keeps an account of it on its own website.

In November the ten year agreement with Rotary International ended and the support stopped. The Standard reported at the time that the home was on the brink of closure with 161 children. The count has moved since, as children are reintegrated or taken on, but the money has not come back.

That is the honest reason this site exists. The home needs a way to be found by people who are not already connected to it through a parish in Italy or a charity in London.

If you are reading this because you want to help

There is no clever pitch here. The home runs a farm, keeps around a hundred children in school, and has had a hole in its budget since November.
